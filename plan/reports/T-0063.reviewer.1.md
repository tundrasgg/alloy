How F1 happens, all from `task/T-0063:spec/arfc-0003-mesh-wire-protocol.md`:

1. Node I rekeys with R and makes S2. The previous session S1 binds VNI `v`, and S2 does not yet bind it.
2. Line 268: R keeps S1's receive key for 10 seconds after the rekey.
3. Lines 421-430: I seals packets for `v` under the newest confirmed session that binds `v`, which is S1. Nothing limits this to the 10 seconds, and I holds S1's send key until 180 seconds after S1's handshake (line 267).
4. Lines 400-404: I sends a bind request only when it has a packet for `v`.
5. So a packet for `v` that arrives more than 10 seconds after the rekey is sealed under S1. R has discarded S1's key and drops the packet (section 6). The same happens to the next packets, until a bind for `v` in S2 completes one round trip later. A VNI that sends one packet every 30 seconds loses about one packet per 120-second rekey cycle. Under the old layout the VNI was inside the frame, so every VNI moved to the new session together and nothing was lost this way.

How F2 happens, from lines 307-311, 108-118 and 777-782:

1. A node holds session S with a peer. Every pending initiation counts toward the cap of 4.
2. The spec does not say that a retried initiation replaces the earlier one. Retry policy is an open question in section 9.
3. With S and three unanswered initiations, a fourth initiation would make a fifth entry. "Oldest" is not defined across sessions and pending initiations. Read by creation time, the oldest is S, so the node discards it.
4. The responder side is the same: S plus one session per answered initiation whose response was lost. After four lost responses the fifth initiation makes the responder discard S. The initiator still seals under S, because no response reached it, so the responder drops all its traffic.
5. Under load, a cookie round leaves an extra initiation pending (3.1), so fewer losses reach the cap.
