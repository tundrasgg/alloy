Model (scratch, not committed, `handover2.py`, about 330 lines). It encodes 3.2, 3.4 and 3.9 as on the branch. Steps are 0.1 s, one-way delay is one step. Node N's old process O holds sessions with 6 peers and none with 3 more. O passes sessions, counters, windows and the 3.2 table to successor S. S serves 0 to 40 s. Each step it seals, accepts the peers' transport, accepts peer initiations (rekeys, or new peers opening a session) and opens sessions to new peers it has traffic for. It records consumed sessions and initiation counters as 3.9 says. With small probabilities it crashes, sometimes between recording and storing an initiation counter. Then O resumes under steps 1 to 3 and the 1 s re-handshake, plus a lazy open (ARFC 0004 section 3.8) when it has traffic for a peer, with probability `q` per step. An attacker replays every captured transport packet and initiation every 5 s. Peers seal under their newest session and rekey at 120 s. The gap of a peer is the time from O resuming to the first packet of that peer that O accepts.

## Issues

- `spec-issue`: `spec/arfc-0003-mesh-wire-protocol.md` 3.9, "State that travels". Pending handshakes are not handed over. A response to an initiation the old process sent reaches the successor, which drops it because no initiation is pending (section 6, cost table). The peer, as responder, already holds the new session. On a handover that succeeds, this can exceed the 100 ms of ARFC 0017 section 3.6. Out of this task's scope ("other handover state").
