## Findings

| id | severity | where | claim | evidence | change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | fix | `spec/arfc-0001-runtime-seams.md:419-430` | Clause 3.13 makes serialized configuration values persisted state but bounds the read window only by what the previous release writes, and nothing rewrites a stored binding at finalization. A binding written at version `p - 1` during one upgrade and never changed becomes unreadable two releases later, and its resource fails with `UnsupportedVersion`. | Attack 2 trace above; clause 3.13 bullets 1 and 2; ARFC 0017 clauses 3.3 and 3.7 (finalize "writes the new formats", with no statement that existing stored values are rewritten); I-18. | ARFC 0001 states either that finalization rewrites every stored binding at the new `VERSION`, or that `MIN_VERSION` covers every version still present in stored bindings, so that no stored binding becomes unreadable through upgrades alone. |
| F2 | fix | `spec/arfc-0005-simulator-and-natlab.md:816` | The new buffering bullet and the `stream-close` how 1 rule (line 1141) depend on when a `mem` connection becomes reset, but no clause states it: not for a peer that drops its `Stream`, a peer host crash, or a partition. In particular, what a `write` does once the reading end is dropped (fail with `ConnectionReset`, fail with `Closed`, or keep writing into a buffer that is discarded) is not stated, and with a full buffer it may wait forever. | Attack 3 grep above; clause 3.1.9 ("Dropping a stream closes both directions", "bytes not yet read are discarded when the reading end is dropped"); section 4.1 `stream-close` emission rule; round 1 F4 required one trace byte string per event sequence. | Clause 3.1.9 states, for `mem`, which events reset a connection end and when, and what `read` and `write` on the other end return after the peer end is dropped or its host crashes, so that no `write` waits forever on a connection nobody can read. |
| F3 | note | `spec/arfc-0005-simulator-and-natlab.md:691` | The product `(w - w0) * (1,000,000 + p)` exceeds `u64` after 3.42 hours and `i64` after 1.71 hours of world time in one rate segment at `p = 500,000`, so a transcription in 64-bit arithmetic overflows in a long run. | Attack 1 output above; `Instant` reaches 2^63 - 1 ns (section 7). | Clause 3.1.4 states that the formula is evaluated without overflow, for example in 128-bit arithmetic, over the whole `Instant` range. |
| F4 | note | `spec/arfc-0005-simulator-and-natlab.md:256` | The doc comment calls 65,535 "the largest UDP payload", but the UDP length field counts the 8-byte header, so the largest payload is 65,527 bytes (65,507 over IPv4). The bound itself is safe. | RFC 768 (Length "is the length in octets of this user datagram including this header"); RFC 791 total length limit of 65,535 including the 20-byte IP header. | The doc comment describes the bound accurately, for example "the largest UDP length field value". |
| F5 | note | `spec/arfc-0005-simulator-and-natlab.md:1129` | "Each open connection of the host" at a crash does not say whether a connection whose end was already reset, or whose write side was shut down, but whose `Stream` was not dropped, counts as open and writes `stream-close` how 3. | Section 4.1 emission rules (lines 1127-1143): how 0 and how 1 do not end the connection's numbering, and "one connection may write several on one host". | Section 4.1 defines "open connection" for the crash rule, for example numbered and not yet dropped by the host. |
