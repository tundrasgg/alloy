## Decisions

- Presets use effective (composed) behavior for double NAT layers; mapping is dependent if either layer is, filtering is the stricter. Reason: keeps the matrix at one row per preset.
- Address-dependent mapping is kept as an axis value but has no preset. Reason: it behaves as address-and-port-dependent against any peer on another IP.

## Issues

- `spec-issue`: `spec/arfc-0004-path-manager.md` section 3.3 is silent on one endpoint-independent side against one endpoint-dependent side. The matrix shows direct for full-cone and addr-restricted against symmetric peers, which needs a probe to be answered at the source it arrived from. Section 3.2 and 3.4 should say so, or the matrix rows become relayed.
- `spec-issue`: `spec/arfc-0005-simulator-and-natlab.md` section 3.4 mapping timeout range is 30 to 300 s. AWS NAT gateway is documented at 350 s, and Richter measured 10 s to 200 s. Widen the range, or state that values clamp.
- `spec-issue`: the keepalive in `spec/arfc-0004-path-manager.md` section 3.4 is 25 s against a 30 s axis floor, a 5 s margin. A preset under 25 s should exist to show the failure path.
- `spec-issue`: `udp-blocked` nodes wait 3 s for UDP replies before using the `stream` underlay (ARFC 0004 section 3.6), which conflicts with relay reachability within 2 s of joining (section 3.2). The matrix needs a separate bound, or one of the two numbers changes.
- `follow-up`: check that nftables and namespaces can build each preset for real mode (ARFC 0005 section 3.5), especially endpoint-independent filtering with a dependent mapping.
- `follow-up`: no published measurement separates home routers by RFC 4787 mapping and filtering. A measurement task or an accepted "unknown" is needed for any frequency-weighted test.
