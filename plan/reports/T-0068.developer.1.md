## Decisions

- N: F1 is enforced by the compiler, not by text matching. A new gate step, `clippy-forbid`, forbids both lints with `--no-deps` and leaves out `alloy-rt`, `alloy-sim`, and `xtask`. The `clippy` step is unchanged and still lints every crate. Why: a forbid cannot be lowered by any attribute, wherever it comes from, so the macro-built, `include!`d, and generated forms are closed too, which no text rule can do. Cost: non-runtime crates are linted twice.
- N: F3 takes the stand-in option. Allowlisting 28 crates would take a research note and a lead decision (`agent/eng/deps.md`). The stand-ins mirror the versions the fixture used: tokio 1.54.1, tokio-macros 2.7.2, rand 0.9.5, rand_core 0.9.5, and getrandom 0.3.4. The stand-in `#[tokio::main]` and `#[tokio::test]` span the builder as `parse_knobs` in tokio-macros 2.7.2 does (`src/entry.rs:444-520`).
- N: `std::thread::current` is banned, since thread identity is not deterministic. The tokio `test-util` clock controls `pause`, `resume`, and `advance` are banned. `tokio::task::Builder` (needs `tokio_unstable`) is listed and recorded in `unresolved`.
- R: `std::thread::sleep_until` and `std::os::windows::fs::junction_point` are left out. Both resolve, but on 1.100.0 a call to either fails with E0658 (unstable library feature), so stable code cannot reach them. `std::os::unix::fs::mkfifo` is left out because whether it exists on Linux could not be checked here.
- R: when the two git readings differ, both blocks are judged by the `footers`, `trailers`, and `task-id` rules. Only the `interpret-trailers --parse` block decides whether `Task:` or `BREAKING CHANGE:` is present, and which ids the path lock uses.
- R: a trailing `# comment` after the trailers is now ignored, as git ignores it. One existing unit-test expectation changed to match: the block is `["Task: T-0001"]`, not `["Task: T-0001", "# comment"]`.
