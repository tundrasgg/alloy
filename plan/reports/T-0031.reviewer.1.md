Model (scratch, not committed, written without the architect's scripts). `natcheck2.py` is about 150 lines. Each NAT has mapping and filtering from {EI, AD, APD}, filtering per mapping, one external address, and a fresh unused port for each new mapping, with no timeouts. Each node first sends to an anchor to learn its reflexive candidate. X starts its probe set at 0 and Y after a signal delay drawn from the seed. Each set is 5 rounds 200 ms apart, with a probing window until 1 s after the fifth round. Each packet's delay is 5 to 60 ms, and the horizon is 5 s. Section 3.2 steps 4 to 8 are applied as written:

- One answer per challenge-bearing message, sent to its source.
- The answer carries its own challenge only when it answers a probe from an unverified endpoint.
- An unverified source is answered only while probing.
- An endpoint is verified by an echo from the endpoint the challenge was sent to, once, within 2 s.

Direct means X verified Y's endpoint ye and Y verified X's endpoint xe, where ye and xe are each other's mappings. `PRFLX=0` probes only the advertised candidate. `PRFLX=1` also probes, in the remaining rounds of the current set, every endpoint the node has verified (step 6 calls these candidates, step 3 probes every candidate pair, and step 7 allows probes to verified endpoints). The rule is section 3.3, cases 1 to 3.

Trace of the first row, with X on (AD mapping, EI filtering) and Y on (AD, AD):

1. Y probes X_r from Y'. X's EI filter admits the probe.
2. X answers from X' to Y' and adds challenge cX, because Y' is unverified.
3. Y answers the answer: echo cX, no challenge. X verifies Y'.
4. In its next round X probes Y', which is now verified and a peer-reflexive candidate.
5. Y answers from Y' with challenge cY, because X' is unverified. X answers with echo cY, and Y verifies X'.

Both sides now hold a round trip on (X', Y'). Section 3.3 puts this pair on the relay.

`layered.py` is the same model with a chain of two devices per side. Every double side is run against every single or double side, and the side is reduced as section 3.3 states. F2 replay, read against the branch text. Step 5 answers a replayed probe, or a replayed answer that carries a challenge, from a spoofed source once, unpadded, and only while the node is probing the sender. A replayed answer without a challenge is never answered. Step 7 forbids probing the spoofed endpoint, because it never echoes a challenge sent to it. This agrees with the architect's `replay.py` ratios (at most 1.0 in packets and bytes for probes). Section 3.4 is different (`spec/arfc-0004-path-manager.md:154-155`, on the branch and on main): "An authenticated packet from a new source endpoint moves the session's return path to that endpoint at once." AL transport packets pass the replay window on a fresh counter (`spec/arfc-0003-mesh-wire-protocol.md` section 3.4). An underlay attacker that drops one transport packet from A and reinjects it with source V makes B send all of its session traffic for A to V. This lasts until A's next packet from its real endpoint, which is up to 25 s when A is idle (the keepalive in section 3.4). ARFC 0020 section 2.1 gives the underlay attacker drop and inject.

F3: ARFC 0004 section 3.4 (`:145-149`) now requires a home session keepalive after 5 s idle. ARFC 0005 section 3.4 derives the `short-timeout` relay expectation from it (5 s against 20 s). ADR 0045's cost of 0.4 packets per second (2 home sessions, one keepalive every 5 s each) is arithmetic that checks out. F3 is resolved.
