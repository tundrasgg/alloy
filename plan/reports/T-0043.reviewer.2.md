Model. It lives in scratch and is not committed. I wrote it from the round 2 text without the architect's or the round 1 reviewer's scripts. `pm2.py` is about 230 lines, with the drivers `classes2.py`, `grid2.py`, `jitter2.py` and `relayattack2.py`. The NATs follow ARFC 0005 section 3.4: mapping and filtering from {EI, AD, APD}, filtering per mapping, one external address, and a fresh port per mapping. A layered side chains two devices, each keyed on the final destination. Each node's one advertised candidate is its mapping toward the anchor. The model applies section 3.2 steps 3 to 9 as written:

- Packets are accepted once.
- The echo is checked before the node decides to answer, and a challenge is used up by its first echo, from any source.
- Verification lapses only when E is not usable, the node is not probing, and 2 s have passed since verification.
- `usable` means verified plus a transport packet accepted directly within 75 s, before or after verification.
- A keepalive goes out on verification and to every verified endpoint in each later round.
- The heard rule of step 9 applies. The RTT rule is not modeled.
- Section 3.4 applies: the trigger from an unverified source, the bound with one deferred set, and the signal.

Every send toward an endpoint that is not verified is checked against section 8. `direct` means both active paths are direct and a packet each side sent would arrive.

Finding F1 (`relayattack2.py`). X is (APD, APD), Y is (APD, APD), the class is `relayed`, and one-way delay is 50 ms. The anchor relays X's transport packets to Y 150 ms after sending. The attacker reads the opaque AL packet in the anchor's envelope to Y (ARFC 0003 section 3.7 authenticates it but does not encrypt it again) and injects it at Y from V. It blocks nothing. Y moves at once, because step 9 makes the usable direct path active "when no direct path is active". Step 8 counts the transport packet from V that arrived before verification. No 1 s silence comes first, and the RTT rule is not involved. On a relayed pair no other usable direct path exists, so Y stays on V until the path fails 75 s after the last packet from V. The 75 s end bound of section 8 holds (10.05 + 75 = 85.05). The start bound does not.
