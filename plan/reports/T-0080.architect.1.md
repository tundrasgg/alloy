## Summary

ARFC 0004 now moves traffic only on an echo of a fresh challenge, for direct paths, relayed paths, and anchors' home sessions. The timing rules (1 s without hearing, the RTT switch, the 75 s transport window, the anchor's 15 s close) are gone. Paths are kept by checks, and a far endpoint fails when its 3 latest challenges go unechoed. Section 7 lists every table with a bound and the behavior at the bound, and nothing is kept for an endpoint that is not verified. ADR 0061 supersedes ADR 0049. A new model reproduces 34 of 81 and 872 of 7,290 with 0 mismatches and the 5 s bound. One part of the goal is not met: redirection by an underlay attacker that reads and injects cannot be made nil. Section 8 says why and gives the bound.

## Changes

  - 3.1.2: an anchor sends to a homed node only at its current endpoint, the one that most recently echoed a fresh challenge. The current endpoint lapses 75 s after the last echo, and the ARFC states what the anchor does then (F4). A node's relayed traffic goes only to published anchor addresses.

  - 3.4: checks replace the 25 s and 75 s rules, and the rebinding text is rewritten.

How each finding of `plan/reports/T-0043.reviewer.2.md` is resolved:

- F1 (block): resolved. Section 8 no longer claims a start condition that does not hold. It now says a node on the relay moves to V at once after one carried exchange and one accepted transport packet. A node on a working direct path moves only after blocking that makes its far endpoint fail (at least 2.4 s). It also states the end bounds (31 s at a node, 25 s or 75 s at an anchor) and the sustain cost: one carried exchange per check. ADR 0061 says the same.
- F2 (fix): moot. Relay envelopes no longer move an anchor. Only an echo in a probe message, which has a replay window, sets the current endpoint. Section 8 says the envelopes' missing replay window moves nothing.
- F3 (block): resolved. A node keeps no record of transport packets from an endpoint that is not verified, and `usable` counts only packets since verification. The anchor "hears" table is gone. Section 7 bounds every table. The class counts and the 5 s bound still hold, because of the keepalive sent when a path becomes usable (model below).
- F4 (fix): resolved. The 15 s close is gone. Without an echo for 75 s the anchor drops only the current endpoint and keeps the session and its keys. It drops envelopes and signaling for the node meanwhile. Delivery resumes at the next reflection exchange, within 25 s and two round trips.

Why the redirection cannot be nil. To verify V, a node needs an echo from V of a challenge it sent to V. An attacker that reads can carry the sealed message to the peer from any source the peer answers, and carry the sealed answer back from V. No field binds the endpoint, and none can: a node behind a NAT does not see the address it is reached at. The carried exchange is the same one case 3 of section 3.3 needs (A answers an answer from the unverified, non-advertised endpoint of B), so a rule that refused it would close case 3. The goal sentence "The redirection an attacker can cause is then nil" holds only for an attacker that cannot read. Section 8 states both.

## Decisions

- The RTT switch between two working direct paths is dropped in version 1. Moving off a working path without a failure is what an attacker exploits.
- Checks replace the kind-0 path keepalive. A probe every 25 s refreshes the NAT the same way, so ADR 0045 and ARFC 0005's `short-timeout` text still hold. A path whose far endpoint stops answering now fails within 31 s instead of 75 s.
- Every reflection answer from an anchor carries a challenge. The anchor's current endpoint lapses 75 s after the last echo. It replaces the 15 s close and the 4-endpoint table.
- The limit on challenges in probes is 1,024 per peer, oldest dropped first. Honest use stays under it while (own candidates × peer's advertised candidates) × 10 rounds per 2 s, plus checks, is below 1,024.
- I set ADR 0049's status line to `superseded by 0061`, outside `touches`. `agent/protocol/decisions.md` requires this when a superseding ADR is written, and the task's scope says 0061 supersedes 0049.
