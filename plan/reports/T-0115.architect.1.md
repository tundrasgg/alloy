## Summary

ARFC 0003 3.3 now defines when a session **follows** another (the node sent the
message that makes it at the peer after the other was confirmed), discards a
session's keys 10 s after the first transport packet under a session that follows
it (180 s and the session limit unchanged), and defines crossed rekeys with a
table of arrival orders. 3.4 bounds sealing under an older session to 5 s after
the first seal under a newer one and drops packets under a discarded `recv_key`.

## Changes

- `spec/arfc-0003-mesh-wire-protocol.md`: 3.3 gains the follows relation, the three discard rules, the proof that rule 2 never drops a key the peer still seals under, crossed rekeys, the arrival order table, and the WireGuard comparison with its source; 3.4 gains the 5 s sender bound, the discarded-key receive check, and a crossed rekey note.

## Decisions

- N: the proof the peer moved past `P` is a transport packet under a session that follows `P`, not one under a session the node orders newer; review F1 (seed 201) is the case where those differ.
- N: the 10 s now runs from that proof, not from the rekey, so a receive key toward a silent peer lives up to the 180 s bound. This is the change review F2 asked to land in the spec first; forward secrecy keeps the 180 s bound, as the probe keys already do.
- N: a node seals nothing under `P` 5 s after it first seals under a newer session, so the 10 s cover 5 s of underlay delay; before, a VNI could stay on the previous session until bound, with no limit.
- R: no wire change and no tie-break. Without a signal the two nodes cannot agree which crossed session is newer, so each keeps both receive keys and they converge on the next handshake that follows both.
