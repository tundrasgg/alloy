Model (scratch, not committed). I wrote it without the architect's scripts. `pm43.py` is about 250 lines, with drivers `classes43.py`, `readings43.py`, `grid43.py`, `trace43.py`, `prflx43.py`, `attack43.py` and `bound43.py`. Each NAT has mapping and filtering from {EI, AD, APD}, filtering per mapping, one external address, a fresh port per new mapping, and no timeouts. A layered side chains two devices, each keyed on the final destination. Each node learns its reflexive candidate from an anchor, and that is its only advertised candidate. X starts a probe set at 0, and Y starts after a signal delay. A set is 5 rounds 200 ms apart, and the node probes until 1 s after the fifth round. Every packet is delayed 5 to 60 ms, or a fixed one-way delay D minus up to 10 ms of jitter. The model applies section 3.2 steps 3 to 8 as written:

- unpadded probes go only to advertised candidates;
- an answer carries its own challenge only for a probe from an unverified source;
- an unverified source is answered only while probing;
- an echo verifies the endpoint it came from, once, within 2 s;
- a keepalive goes out on verification and to every verified endpoint in each later round;
- verification lapses at the end of probing unless a usable path uses the endpoint;
- `usable` means verified plus a direct transport packet since the last probe set start;
- a direct transport packet from an unverified source starts a signaled probe set when the node is not probing;
- the rate bound is 2 per 5 s and 20 per 5 min, with one deferred set.

Every send toward an unverified endpoint is checked against section 8 items 1 and 2. `direct` means each side holds a usable path and the two endpoints are each other's mappings. Three readings of the text are switchable: `strict` (the transport packet must arrive after E is verified), `literal` (any transport packet from E since the probe set started), and `DROPFIRST` (a message from an unverified source is dropped before its echo is checked when the node is not probing).

The failing combinations at D=0.5 s are all in cases 2 and 3, for example (EI,EI) with (AD,EI), and (AD,EI) with (AD,EI). Most end `mixed` at 5 s. The architect's `lat43.py` reports 0 of 34 at one-way 0.50 s and signal 0.75 s. That does not reproduce. Here is the mechanism (`trace43.py EI/EI AD/EI 0 5 0.5 0.5`). X, the EI side, never probes Y's peer-reflexive endpoint Y' (step 6). So X verifies Y' only through the echo of a challenge X put in an answer to Y's probe. Y's first probe at t_Y reaches X at t_Y + D. X's answer reaches Y at t_Y + 2D. Y's echo reaches X at t_Y + 3D, and X verifies Y' and sends its first keepalive, which reaches Y at t_Y + 4D. Y's verification of X lapses at t_Y + 1.8 s, the end of its probing. In the trace, Y lapses at 2.300 and X's keepalive arrives at 2.479. So for D > 0.45 s, Y cannot become usable in the first pair of probe sets, whatever S is. Recovery then depends on probe sets that section 3.4 starts, at most 2 per 5 s.

## Issues

- `follow-up`: I confirm the architect's `spec-issue` on `spec/arfc-0004-path-manager.md:49-51` and `:302-304`. An anchor's traffic to a homed node's verified endpoint ends only when the home session closes, and no spec defines when that happens. Section 3.4's 75 s failure rule names direct paths only.
- `follow-up`: Finding F1 does not depend on probe replay alone. With replay protection for probes (T-0038), part 1 still works if the attacker races a fresh X probe, and part 2 still works with a fresh reflection request. T-0038 should not be taken as closing F1.
