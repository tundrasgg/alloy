## Summary

ARFC 0004 sections 3.1.1, 3.1.2, 3.2 steps 4 to 9, 3.4, 3.5, 7 and 8 are revised again, and ADR 0049 is rewritten to match. Only accepted packets count, and a challenge is used up by its first echo. A node or anchor leaves the endpoint it sends to only after 1 s without a transport packet from it. Section 8 now states the redirection an attacker that overtakes packets can cause, bounded in kind, rate and duration, and claims nothing beyond it (F1). A 2 s floor on verification makes the 5 s bound hold at the stated 750 ms signal and 500 ms one-way delays (F2). Steps 5 and 8 each have one reading (F3), and every required packet is in a list (F4). A new model reproduces 34 of 81 and 872 of 7,290 with 0 mismatches.

## Changes

- `spec/arfc-0004-path-manager.md`: 3.1.1 a reflection request is a probe message and only accepted ones are answered. 3.1.2 the reflexive candidate comes from the answer to the latest request. Anchors hear an endpoint by its transport packets and envelopes, send to the one heard most recently, keep the current one while it is heard within 1 s, keep 4 per homed node, and close a home session after 15 s of silence. 3.2 step 4 defines `accepted`. How each finding of `plan/reports/T-0043.reviewer.1.md` is resolved:

- F1 (block): the reviewer was right. No probe field binds an address, and no node can see the address its peer reaches it at behind a NAT. So verification cannot be made sound against an attacker that gets copies accepted in place of the genuine packets, and the ARFC now bounds and states what such an attacker can cause. Section 8 says that copies arriving after the genuine packets change nothing (0 packets to V above). It also says what V can receive when the attacker overtakes packets or delivers dropped ones: only the packets the node would send that peer, or the anchor that homed node, at their own rate. The start needs 1 s without the real endpoint being heard. The end comes 1 s after the last packet from V once the real endpoint is heard again, at most 5 s later at an anchor (25 s if displaced) and 75 s at a node. The trace shows the reflexive candidate can also be poisoned, and the section bounds that to item 1 for up to 25 s. ADR 0049 claims no more. The reviewer's part 2 no longer works without blocking: the genuine echo now uses up challenge 102 (step 6), and the anchor does not move while it hears the node.
- F2 (block): the reviewer was right. The 5 s bound keeps its delays, and the probing window now holds at them. Verification lasts at least 2 s (step 6), longer than the 2D from verification to the confirming keepalive at D = 0.5 s. The grid gives 0 of 34 single and 0 of 872 layered failures up to D = 0.5 s and S = 0.75 s, latest 2.75 s. The text states the S + 4D reasoning.
- F3 (fix): the reviewer was right. Step 8 now reads: a transport packet "before or after E was verified". Step 5 checks the echo first, then decides whether to answer. Under the strict reading 20 to 30 of 34 fail, so that reading is excluded in words.
- F4 (fix): the reviewer was right. Step 6 no longer lists what a peer-reflexive candidate receives. In every other respect it is a verified endpoint. `Session traffic` (3.4) covers transport packets, the 25 s keepalive and handshake messages. Step 7 and section 8 add handshake initiations to verified endpoints only, and handshake replies to the initiation's source. Section 8 states that sends to an anchor's published address and on a stream connection the node opened are outside the list, which covers the initiation and reflection requests to an anchor.

## Decisions

- Redirection is bounded and stated, not prevented. Reason: no node observes the address it is reached at, and binding the address would need an ARFC 0003 wire change, which is out of this task's touches.
- The heard rule replaces the move without hysteresis, at nodes and at anchors. The node keeps its active direct path, or the anchor its endpoint, while it heard the far endpoint within 1 s, and otherwise moves to the one heard most recently. Reason: an attacker that does not block cannot move a path that is in use, and the end of any redirection is bounded. Cost: rebinding recovery is now up to 1 s after the old endpoint goes silent (0.82 s in the model, 0.40 s in round 1).
- `usable` no longer resets when a probe set starts. It lasts while a transport packet arrived within 75 s, and failure is defined on transport packets. Reason: the reset let a few overtaken packets move a path still heard (the `RESET` trace).
- Verification has a 2 s floor after it is made. Reason: it makes the 5 s bound hold at the stated delays with the first pair of probe sets. The floor changes no sends: answers to verified endpoints and step 8 keepalives only.
- An anchor keeps at most 4 verified endpoints per homed node and closes a home session after 15 s with no accepted packet (3 home keepalive periods). Reason: I-21, and the round 1 `spec-issue` that no spec ends an anchor's traffic to a node that has gone.
- A node's reflexive candidate comes from the answer to its latest request. Reason: one rule. The poisoning that remains is stated in section 8.

## Issues

- `spec-issue`: `spec/arfc-0020-security-model.md` sections 3.2 and 8 do not state the redirection that `spec/arfc-0004-path-manager.md` section 8 now bounds. An underlay attacker that overtakes packets can send a node's traffic for one peer to an address it picks, for as long as it keeps doing so. ARFC 0020 should list this under "Not defended in version 1", or say that 3.2's "block traffic" includes it. Not in this task's touches.
- `spec-issue`: `spec/arfc-0003-mesh-wire-protocol.md` section 3.7 gives the relay envelope a counter but states no replay window. ARFC 0004 section 3.1.2 counts an envelope at an anchor only when it is accepted, so section 3.7 should state the check, as 3.4 and 3.6 do.
- `follow-up`: `spec/arfc-0005-simulator-and-natlab.md` section 3.4 (the `short-timeout` expectation) speaks of "an authenticated packet" on the path. ARFC 0004 section 3.4 now defines failure by transport packets. No contradiction, but the lab check should count transport packets.
- `follow-up`: the NAT models (T-0011), the matrix (T-0012) and the conformance suites need the round 2 musts: accepted-only, echo first, challenge used up by its first echo, the 2 s verification floor, the 75 s `usable` window, the heard rule with its 1 s at nodes and anchors, 4 endpoints per homed node, the 15 s home session close, and the section 8 bounds.
- `follow-up`: from the round 1 report and still open: the probe set bound is per peer, so a compromised node with sessions to many nodes multiplies the probes toward a third party it advertises. Poisoning a reflexive candidate (section 8) has the same reach.
