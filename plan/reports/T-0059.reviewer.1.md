Model 1 (scratch, not committed, `handover.py`, about 200 lines). It encodes the 3.4, 3.6 and 3.9 text. Node N's old process O exchanges messages with peer P under sessions with per-key counters and 2,048 windows. At the handover O passes every session, with its counters and windows, to successor S and stops. S seals, accepts, answers P's rekey initiations and accepts attacker replays for 0 to 40 steps. With probability 0.2 it records a session consumed and crashes before using it. Then S ends and O resumes. An underlay attacker replays 5 captured messages every step and every captured message at the end. Mode "main" resumes every session with O's own state. Mode "branch" applies 3.9: S records a session consumed before it seals, accepts or sends a handshake message to P, and O discards consumed sessions and re-handshakes. The 3.2 responder table (highest initiator counter) is not handed back, as 3.9 says nothing of it.

Model 2 (scratch, not committed, `rekey.py`, about 180 lines). It encodes the 3.3 and 3.6 text. Two nodes, 1 s steps, 1,500 steps per seed. Handshake messages and probes are lost with probability `loss` and take 1 step, or 1 to 3 steps with reordering. A node rekeys when its newest session is 120 s old (0 to 2 s jitter, so both sides sometimes rekey at once). An initiator retries after 5 s without a response. Keys are discarded at 180 s. Each node probes with probability 0.5 per step, or node A with 0.01 (a silent peer). A failure is a delivered probe that its receiver drops although both nodes held one session when it was sent. Mode "main" seals under the newest session held and keeps the previous receive key 10 s. Mode "branch" applies the 3.6 session choice and retention rules.

Most of main's drops come from simultaneous rekeys. Each node seals under the session it made last, and the peer drops its copy of that session's receive key 10 s after a newer one exists. The branch has no drops with in-order delivery when both sides probe.

The branch drops have two causes. I found them by printing node state at the first drop of a seed, and by counting drops by whether the sending session was confirmed:

- Reordering (delay 1 to 3 steps, all 3,103 under a confirmed session). A receiver erases the older `probe_recv_key` at its first probe accepted under a newer session (3.6 `:321-325`). An older probe that arrives after that is dropped. This is F6.
- A silent peer (A probes with probability 0.01). In seeds 2 and 5 the sender seals under its newest confirmed session until its own 180 s discard. The peer answered that handshake, so it made the session one delay earlier and discarded it first. A newer session both nodes hold is still unconfirmed because the peer sent nothing under it. In seed 3 a responder has confirmed nothing, because the peer never used its sessions. It falls back to its newest session, whose response was lost. Both cases need an initiator that sends nothing under the sessions it opens. The model allows that, but 3.3 rekeys "after 120 seconds of use". Real delays are also milliseconds, not the model's 1 s step. No finding.

Text trace for F1 (finding): 3.9 opens (`:457-460`) by keeping "the replay protection of ARFC 0020 section 3.2 across that handover, whether it succeeds or fails". ARFC 0017 section 6 (`spec/arfc-0017-upgrades-and-versioning.md:70-75`) and ADR 0057 (`:26-27`) say no message is accepted twice. 3.2 (`:188-192`) has the responder store the highest initiation counter per initiator, and does not say that the store is persistent. 3.9 does not hand that store over, and does not refresh it when the old process resumes. Model 1 keeps the store in process memory. The old process then accepts again an initiation its successor accepted, in 367 of 1,000 seeds, on the branch as on main.

Text trace for F2 (finding): 3.9 at `:496-501` opens a new session with a peer whose newest session was discarded only "when it next has a message for that peer", or within 1 s for a home session. Until then the peer's messages "are dropped as naming no session". The peer does not learn of the discard. ARFC 0004 section 3.4 (`spec/arfc-0004-path-manager.md:134-136`) sends a keepalive on a direct path only after 25 s with nothing sent. A peer reached through a relay gets no keepalive (only home sessions do, `:145-148`), so the gap ends at the peer's own rekey, 120 s after its handshake (3.3, `:250`). ARFC 0017 section 3.6 (`spec/arfc-0017-upgrades-and-versioning.md:52-53`) allows no network gap over 100 ms during handover (I-17).

Text trace for F3 (finding): 3.7 (`:444-448`) gives the relay envelope an 8-byte `counter` and a tag under "the session key between the node and the anchor". 3.9 at `:476-477` names relay envelopes among the messages sealed under a key of a session. "State that travels" (`:462-467`) lists the transport send counters and windows and the probe counter and window, and "a key is never passed without that state", but it does not list the envelope counter or any replay state an anchor keeps for envelopes. A successor that receives a session without the envelope counter starts it again, on a successful handover too, where the consumed rule does not apply.

## Issues

- `spec-issue`: `spec/arfc-0003-mesh-wire-protocol.md` 3.6, `:303-327`. "Newest" is not defined. In a simultaneous rekey the two nodes order their two new sessions differently (each made the one it answered first). Model 2 shows the rules still work under local creation order, but implementations must agree on what "newest" means for a test to be written.
- `follow-up`: D-0024 (T-0063) removes the transport `queue` field. 3.4 and 3.9 at `:464-465` speak of the counters and windows "of every queue". T-0063 must carry 3.9 over to one receiver index per queue.
