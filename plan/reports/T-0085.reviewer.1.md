- Attack 1 (seeds 1 to 2,000): random static keys, ephemerals, payloads and psks. One third of the seeds use the zero psk. Each seed tries:
  - a random bit flip in the initiation;
  - three random 52-byte forged responses;
  - a random bit flip in the genuine response;
  - a one-bit-off psk.
  Every one is rejected, and each rejection hands the handshake back. The genuine response then completes it. The two sides' keys seal and open in both directions. At the same `(queue, counter)`, the two directions give different ciphertexts (I-07).
- Attack 2 (seeds 1 to 500): two handshakes are pending to one responder. Each response is fed to the other handshake, and then to a third, fresh handshake. All are rejected, and each handshake still completes with its own response. Keys of different sessions do not open each other's packets.
- Attack 3 (seeds 1 to 1,000): random queue below 64, counter below 2^31, frame of 1 to 200 bytes. A packet opens only under its exact queue, full counter and header. It does not open under any of these:
  - `queue ^ 1` or `queue + 256`;
  - `counter ^ 1`, `counter + 2^32` or `counter | 2^63`;
  - the sender's own `recv` key;
  - a one-byte truncation or extension.

Invariants reached:

- I-06: `open_in_place` and `verify_mac1` change no state.
- I-07: seal refuses `counter >= 2^31` and `queue >= 64`. Each handshake cipher key is used once at `n = 0`. Attacks 1 and 3 show the directions are separate.
- I-21: no unbounded buffer.
- I-22: every key type's `Debug` output is tested, and no error variant carries data.

No violation was found.
