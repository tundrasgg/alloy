## Goal

`alloy-link` runs the AL handshake and session lifecycle of ARFC 0003 sections 3.1 to 3.5 as sans-I/O state machines over `alloy-wire` and `alloy-crypto`. The node channel of M11 rung 2 and the mesh of rung 3 send authenticated, encrypted datagrams through it.

## Scope

- Read only the named sections of the long files: ARFC 0003 sections 2, 3.1 to 3.5, 6, and 7.
- The initiator and responder handshake flows of section 3.1:
  - initiation, response, and cookie reply;
  - `mac1` and `mac2` checks before any DH;
  - load-based cookie replies;
  - retries with the section's timers;
  - the handshake replay protection of section 3.2.
- Sessions per peer (section 3.3):
  - keys from `alloy-crypto`, rekey and expiry timers, the per-peer session cap;
  - receiver index allocation in blocks per VNI (ADR 0058);
  - erasure on discard.
- Transport (section 3.4):
  - send and receive per VNI, counter reconstruction and the replay window (`alloy_wire::reconstruct_counter`);
  - inner frames.
- Fragmentation (section 3.5).
- The section 6 failure behavior and every section 7 limit, each with a test at the limit (I-21).
- Time and randomness come in as inputs: an `alloy-rt` clock instant and random bytes passed by the caller, or the `alloy-rt` traits. No direct OS calls. The crate is deterministic for a given input sequence.
- The public interface is a class N decision of this task (D-0029 pattern). Document it in the crate and list it in the report, because the rung 2 node channel and the rung 3 mesh build on it.
- Tests:
  - two state machines driven against each other in memory: handshake, data both ways, rekey, loss, reordering, replay rejection;
  - every drop rule of sections 3.1 and 3.4 exercised;
  - the T-0086 handshake vectors in `tests/conformance/wire` still pass through the real flow.

## Out of scope

- Probes, paths, and relays (sections 3.6 and 3.7, `alloy-path` and `alloy-relay`).
- Stream framing (section 3.8).
- Process handover (section 3.9).
- Sockets and TUN.
